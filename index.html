Everything above is the record. What follows is my read of it — dated, signed, and left up whether it ages well or not.

I / THE PROMISES

A pitch, not a forecast

My starting point: this valuation is largely based on promises, and whether Musk keeps them is unknown given his track record. That record cuts both ways. Betting against his engineering has repeatedly cost people money, but his timelines rarely land when promised, and the S-1 itself ties a grant of up to billion additional shares to milestones including a million-resident Mars colony. A addressable market with ~% of it in AI is not a forecast; it is a pitch.

II / THE PRICE

The was roughly right

Despite this, I think the offering was relatively well priced as of the first few weeks. The first-day close left about on the table, which is small by mega-IPO standards, and a month on the stock still sits above the offer. As a piece of execution, the fixed price was roughly right.

III / THE TAPE

The aftermarket traded the story

The aftermarket is a different matter. The run to and the give-back look to me largely driven by media hype and the 'Musk Story' rather than by anything that changed in the business, amplified by a float of only ~%. In the future I would expect a volatile stock similar to Tesla, partly because Musk is controversial and partly because the price stays reliant on promises. If, for example, it turns out that data centres in space are not viable, I don't see how the stock value couldn't drop, because outside Starlink the underlying isn't really 'real' per se. Starlink is real: of revenue at EBITDA margins. It does not carry a near-two-trillion-dollar company on its own. The rest is expectation, and I think the underlying assets and expectations may not be held to account for a long time, because the marginal buyer here is reactive and follows the story: % of the deal went to retail, and the index funds were engineered in.

IV / THE RANGE

What I would pay

At the last close the market is implying a value of about on the offering share base. Hold that price, assume the multiple normalises to 25× EBITDA by 2030, and the business needs roughly of EBITDA by then: a compound growth rate of about a year for five years from 2025's . I will not underwrite that on promises. I would underwrite something closer to 50 to 55% a year, which is still hyper-growth, and still assumes Starlink keeps compounding and some of the AI story lands. At the same 25× exit that puts my range at roughly to a share. The offer sat just above it, close enough to call efficient; the aftermarket did not. So I am not a buyer at today's price, I would be interested near the top of my range, and if the lockup waves push it below the bottom I would expect to be buying volatility rather than chasing it. If data centres in space stall, all of this is too high, and I accept that being on the record means being wrong in public.
